Check 1 · the program

CAAHEP accreditation

The sonography program itself is reviewed by CAAHEP. Graduates of a CAAHEP-accredited program can sit for the ARDMS registry exams when they finish school. Every school in the main list has it.

Check 2 · the school

Institutional accreditation

The college as a whole is reviewed by a regional accreditor (Middle States, New England, or Southern). This keeps credits transferable and federal financial aid available.

What the degree leads to

Registry credentials

Students pass the SPI physics exam plus a specialty exam. General (abdomen, OB/GYN) leads to RDMS, cardiac (adult echo) to RDCS, vascular to RVT.

Questions to ask on a visit

The answers separate programs more than the brochure does.

  1. When do I apply to the sonography program itself: as a freshman, or after my first two years? How many apply and how many get a seat?
  2. If I don't get a seat in the clinical years, what happens to my credits and my major?
  3. What were your last three years of registry pass rates (SPI and specialty) and job placement?
  4. Which hospitals are your clinical sites, how far are they, and do I need a car?
  5. How many hours a week of clinical work, and does it include nights, weekends, or summers?
  6. Do students take the SPI exam before graduating? Does the program pay for it?
  7. Which tracks can I add: cardiac, vascular, breast, pediatric? Does adding one take an extra term?
  8. What does the clinical year cost beyond tuition (scrubs, background check, travel, exam fees)?
  9. Is housing guaranteed all four years, including the clinical year?
  10. What scholarships are set aside for health-science students?
