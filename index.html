What is sonography?

Sonographers use high-frequency sound waves to make pictures of the inside of the body. Doctors read those pictures to find problems in organs, babies before birth, the heart, and blood vessels. [bls.gov]

What a sonographer does all day

  • Explains the scan to the patient and answers questions
  • Positions the patient and runs the ultrasound machine to get clear pictures
  • Checks that the pictures cover what the doctor needs, and spots anything that looks abnormal
  • Gives the doctor a summary of the findings and keeps patient records
[bls.gov]

Where they work: hospitals (58% of jobs), doctors' offices (21%), medical and diagnostic labs (11%), and outpatient care centers (5%). [bls.gov]

The kinds of sonography jobs

  • Abdominal: the liver, kidneys, gallbladder, and other organs in the belly
  • OB/GYN: pregnancy and the female reproductive system
  • Cardiac (echocardiographer): the heart
  • Vascular: blood vessels and blood flow, such as blocked arteries or clots
  • Breast, musculoskeletal (muscles, tendons, joints), and pediatric (children and babies)
[bls.gov]

Each specialty has its own registry exam. General (abdomen, OB/GYN) leads to the RDMS, cardiac to the RDCS, and vascular to the RVT. [ardms.org] The schools on this page list which tracks they offer.

The classes, from high school to the hospital

  • In high school: Algebra 1 and 2, Geometry, Biology, and Chemistry are required at MCPHS, which also recommends four years of math and lab science, plus Anatomy and Physiology or Physics if offered. [mcphs.edu]
  • First college courses (prerequisites): English composition, precalculus, physics, and Anatomy and Physiology I and II, as in CT State's list. [ctstate.edu]
  • Sonography courses (MCPHS's accredited bachelor's as an example): sonographic physics and instruments, abdominal sonography, OB/GYN sonography, breast, vascular, pediatric, and musculoskeletal sonography, plus scanning labs where students practice on each other. [mcphs.edu]
  • Clinical training: scanning real patients in hospitals and clinics next to working sonographers. At MCPHS that is a full clinical year: three 8-credit clinical courses. [mcphs.edu]

Expect a lot of science and physics. The physics course is what the SPI registry exam tests.

A 2-year Associate in Science in Diagnostic Medical Sonography. Graduates take the same ARDMS registry exams as bachelor's graduates, and can apply for them 60 days before graduation. [ctstate.edu]

The program

  • 24 months of full-time daytime study; no evening, weekend, or online classes. It starts in summer. [ctstate.edu]
  • Clinical training at hospitals around Connecticut; students pay their own travel. [ctstate.edu]
  • Extra costs: background checks, drug screens, a physical and shots, CPR, uniforms, liability insurance, onboarding fees, and exam fees. Failing a check can keep a student out of a clinical site. [ctstate.edu]

Getting in

  • Before applying: C or better in English Composition, Precalculus, Physics for Life Science, and Anatomy & Physiology I and II (A&P II can be in progress that spring); a sonography admission GPA of 2.7 or higher; and a program information session within the past year. [ctstate.edu]
  • Eligible applicants are invited to a required interview (MMI). Applications are open Oct. 1 to Feb. 1. [ctstate.edu]
  • Timeline: take the required courses first, apply Oct. 1 to Feb. 1, start that summer.

Cost

  • CT State's Mary Ann Handley Award (formerly PACT) is for Connecticut residents of any income who take 6+ credits a term and file the FAFSA. It pays the tuition and mandatory fees left after grants, for the first 72 credits. [ctstate.edu]
  • It covers fall and spring only, not summer. The sonography program starts in summer, so ask what summer terms cost. [ctstate.edu]

Ask CT State

  • What were your last three years of registry pass rates and job placement? (Posted on the program page.)
  • After the associate, which bachelor's completion programs do your graduates use?
  • How many applied last year for the 14 seats?

Connecticut also has CAAHEP-accredited hospital certificate programs at Yale New Haven Hospital and Saint Francis Hospital (Hartford). Ask them what you need before applying. [caahep.org]

Counselor's note

Is sonography a fit?

Check this before you apply. The physical demands are written into every program's rules, and finding a poor fit in junior year costs two years and a lot of money.

The work

  • Most of the job happens at an ultrasound machine in a dimly lit room.
  • Sonographers may stand for long periods and lift or turn patients who are ill or disabled.
  • Shifts can include evenings, weekends, or overnights.
[bls.gov]

What programs expect your body to do

CT State's technical standards are a typical example. Students must be able to:

  • Lift 50 pounds routinely, and push or pull large equipment
  • Stand for long periods, up to 80% of the time, and bend or stoop again and again
  • Have full use of hands, wrists, and shoulders, and hold an arm in one position while scanning
  • See small details in dim light, hear patients, and talk with them clearly and kindly
[ctstate.edu]

If she has a disability or health condition, read each school's technical standards and contact its disability office early. CT State says accommodations must be in place before the program starts.

Try it before you commit

  • Ask a hospital imaging department near home whether she can shadow a sonographer for a day.
  • Go to a program information session and ask to see the scanning lab.
  • Read What is sonography? together, then talk about which specialty sounds interesting.

Registry and license basics

  • Graduates of a CAAHEP-accredited program qualify for the ARDMS exams right away (Pathway 2). Students in paperless programs can apply up to 60 days before graduation. [inteleos.org]
  • The SPI physics exam costs $275 and has about 110 questions over two hours. You must pass the SPI and a specialty exam within five years of each other, in either order. [inteleos.org]
  • Connecticut does not require a state license for sonographers. California, Washington DC, New Hampshire, New Mexico, and Oregon do, per MCPHS's licensure list. [mcphs.edu]

Counselor's note

Paying for it

The prices on this page are for one year. What matters is the total for the whole degree, after grants, and how much of it the family can borrow.

What the degree really costs

  • Multiply the yearly price by the years you will be billed: 4 years at most schools, 3 year-round years at MCPHS Boston. The clinical year is billed too.
  • The net prices on this page are federal averages from 2023–24. Your family's number can be very different.
  • Every school's website has a net price calculator (search the site for "net price calculator"). Run it for each school on the shortlist with your real numbers and write the result next to the school.
  • Ask each school: Is my scholarship renewed every year, and what GPA keeps it?

How much the family can borrow (2026–27)

  • In the student's name: up to $5,500 the first year, $6,500 the second, and $7,500 each year after; $31,000 in all. Interest is 6.52%, plus a 1.057% fee. [studentaid.gov] [studentaid.gov]
  • In a parent's name (Parent PLUS): new limits started July 1, 2026: no more than $20,000 a year and $65,000 in all for each student. Interest is 9.07%. Parent PLUS loans taken after July 1, 2026 can only be repaid on the Tiered Standard plan, with no income-driven plans and no Public Service Loan Forgiveness. [studentaid.gov]
  • Example: a $40,000-a-year net price is about $160,000 for four years. Federal loans can cover at most $27,000 (student) + $65,000 (parent) = $92,000. The other $68,000 has to come from savings, income, more scholarships, or private loans.

Connecticut money

  • File the 2027–28 FAFSA at studentaid.gov. Connecticut's priority date is Feb. 15, 2027. [studentaid.gov]
  • Roberta B. Willis Scholarship: only for Connecticut residents at a four-year Connecticut public or nonprofit private college, like Sacred Heart or Quinnipiac on this page. Up to $5,250 a year (need plus merit: top 20% of the junior class and/or SAT 1200+ or ACT 25+) or up to $4,500 (need-based). FAFSA by Feb. 15; the high school counselor submits the application. It does not follow her to a college in another state. [portal.ct.gov]

A regional discount: Rhode Island College

RIC gives students from Connecticut (and MA, ME, NH, VT, NY, NJ) its Northeast Neighbors discount, 40% off out-of-state tuition. [ric.edu]

  • 2026–27 tuition and fees: $17,353 for a Northeast Neighbor, versus $29,261 out-of-state ($12,123 for Rhode Island residents). Medical Imaging adds $550 a year. Housing and meals: $15,108. [ric.edu]
  • RIC's sonography years are at Brown University Health's School of Medical Imaging, by separate application. See the RIC card.

Counselor's note

What happens if you go to one of these schools

The goal of any sonography degree is passing the ARDMS registry exams, because employers may require or prefer that certification. [bls.gov] Where a program stands with CAAHEP decides which route to those exams a graduate uses. [ARDMS rules]

The bachelor's has no CAAHEP accreditation

Sacred Heart, Quinnipiac, SUNY Upstate, Eastern International.

  • She can still take the registry exams, by ARDMS Pathway 3B: bachelor's students qualify after at least 1,680 hours of clinical scanning over 12 months inside the program, and can apply up to a year before finishing.
  • Accreditation is not back-dated. If the program is accredited only after she graduates, she still counts as a graduate of an unaccredited program.
  • Ask: How many clinical hours do your students complete? Which pathway do your graduates use, and how many passed last year?

An accredited associate sits inside the bachelor's

Mount Aloysius, South College, Eastwick.

  • Graduates of a CAAHEP-accredited program use Pathway 2: no extra clinical experience, exams right at graduation.
  • So the key question is whether bachelor's students finish that same accredited sequence. If yes, she is a graduate of an accredited program. If not, she is in the first group.
  • Ask: Do bachelor's students complete the accredited program, and can they take the exams at graduation?

2+2 and 3+1 with an accredited partner

Rhode Island College, PennWest Clarion, IUP, York College of PA, Commonwealth U, Immaculata, Notre Dame of Maryland.

  • The sonography training happens in the partner's CAAHEP-accredited program. Finishing it makes her a graduate of an accredited program (Pathway 2).
  • The catch: getting into the partner is a separate application, and these schools say a seat is not guaranteed.
  • Ask: What share of your students got a partner seat last year? If I don't get one, what degree do I finish with?

The bottom line

  • A CAAHEP-accredited bachelor's (the main list) is the most direct route: exams at graduation.
  • The other routes can work, but each adds a condition: enough clinical hours, finishing the accredited part, or winning a partner seat.
  • Get the answer in writing from the program before she commits.

Questions to ask on a visit

The answers separate programs more than the brochure does.

  1. When do I apply to the sonography program itself: as a freshman, or after my first two years? How many apply and how many get a seat?
  2. If I don't get a seat in the clinical years, what happens to my credits and my major?
  3. What were your last three years of registry pass rates (SPI and specialty) and job placement?
  4. Which hospitals are your clinical sites, how far are they, and do I need a car?
  5. How many hours a week of clinical work, and does it include nights, weekends, or summers?
  6. Do students take the SPI exam before graduating? Does the program pay for it?
  7. Which tracks can I add: cardiac, vascular, breast, pediatric? Does adding one take an extra term?
  8. What does the clinical year cost beyond tuition (scrubs, background check, travel, exam fees)?
  9. Is housing guaranteed all four years, including the clinical year?
  10. What scholarships are set aside for health-science students?
  11. Do AP or UConn Early College Experience credits count toward anatomy, physics, or math prerequisites?
  12. How do you count job placement: all graduates, or only those looking for sonography jobs?
  13. Why did students leave the program in the last three years?
  14. Do I need a car for clinical training? Can I ask for a site near home in Connecticut?
  15. What does the clinical year cost in total: background check, drug screen, physical and shots, uniforms, insurance, travel, and housing near the site?
  16. Is there free tutoring for anatomy, physics, and math? How do I set up disability accommodations, and what documents do you need?
  17. Can I see your annual campus security report? Is there a safe-walk or shuttle service at night, and do clinical shifts end after dark?
  18. If sonography isn't right for me, which majors can I switch to without losing a year?
